The term **“prompt injection” was first used in May 2022 by the Twitter/X user @himbodhisattva**. **Simon Willison independently proposed the term in September 2022 and helped popularize it**, which is why he is sometimes credited with coining it.

**Difference from jailbreaking:**

- **Prompt injection** is the broader attack: malicious instructions are inserted into a prompt or into content the model processes, exploiting its difficulty distinguishing trusted instructions from untrusted data. This includes **indirect attacks** hidden in webpages, emails, or documents.
- **Jailbreaking** specifically means bypassing a model’s safety rules or content safeguards.

They can overlap—a prompt injection may be used to jailbreak a model—but they are not synonyms. Prompt injection can also manipulate an application into leaking data, misusing tools, or changing its output without necessarily defeating safety safeguards.
