The term **“prompt injection” was first used, in the AI-security sense, by the Twitter/X user @himbodhisattva in May 2022**. **Simon Willison independently proposed and popularized the term in September 2022**, comparing the attack to SQL injection. Thus, Willison is often credited with popularizing—not strictly inventing—the phrase.

**Difference from jailbreaking:**

- **Prompt injection** exploits an LLM’s failure to distinguish trusted instructions from untrusted input. For example, text in a document or user message says, “Ignore the instructions above,” causing the model to perform an unintended task. It can be **direct** (user input) or **indirect** (instructions hidden in a webpage, email, or file).
- **Jailbreaking** specifically aims to **bypass a model’s safety restrictions**—for example, persuading it to provide content it is designed to refuse.

They overlap: a prompt injection can be used as a jailbreak, but prompt injection can also cause unintended behavior without bypassing safety filters.
